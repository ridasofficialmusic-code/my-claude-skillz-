## Sources behind the script
- Liikkanen, L. A. (2012). Musical activities predispositing to involuntary musical imagery. *Psychology of Music*: about 89% of respondents reported earworms at least weekly.
- Jakubowski, K., Finkel, S., Stewart, L., & Müllensiefen, D. (2017). Dissecting an earworm: Melodic features and song popularity predict involuntary musical imagery. *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts*: catchy features are faster tempo, common melodic shapes and an unusual interval.
- Hyman, I. E., et al. (2013). Going Gaga: Investigating, creating, and manipulating the song stuck in my head. *Applied Cognitive Psychology*: earworms appear during low-load tasks, and moderately hard tasks like anagrams reduce them.
- Beaman, C. P., Powell, K., & Rapley, E. (2015). Want to block earworms from conscious awareness? B(u)y gum! *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*.

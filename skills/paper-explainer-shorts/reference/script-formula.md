# Retention script formula (60 s)

Budget: **140–160 words** across 14–17 short lines. One idea per line. Spoken English, with contractions and short words.

| Scene | Time | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 hook | 0–10 s | Stop the scroll, open a loop | **Relatable moment** (2nd person, specific detail) → **"Why does it do this?"** → **reassure + tease**: "It's not random. Your brain thinks it's helping you." |
| 1 reason #1 | 10–22 s | First payoff | "Reason number one: *[3–4 word headline]*." → mechanism in plain words (name **one** brain part or term at most) → consequence |
| 2 turn | ~22–24 s | Pattern interrupt, re-hook | "But that's not even the scary part." / "But that's not what keeps it alive." |
| 3 reason #2 | 24–34 s | Second payoff, usually behavioural | "Reason number two: *[headline]*." → what you do that makes it worse → the brain's "logic" in quotes |
| 4 fix / good news | 34–51 s | Value + surprise | "Now here's the good news." / "So here's the fix." → named effect or study → **one concrete number** → reassuring punchline |
| 5 CTA | 51–58 s | Comments + follow | A question that's easy to answer about the viewer's own life ("What's yours? Tell me in the comments.") → "And follow for more weird science of being human." |

## Hook rules
- The first line is about **the viewer**, in a **specific scene** (time, place, detail): "It's 2am…", "You walk into the kitchen…", "Your summers as a kid…".
- Say the question out loud within 7 s.
- Never open with "Did you know", "Today we'll learn" or your name.
- The hook visual is the thumbnail. Design them together.

## Retention devices to include
- Numbered structure ("Reason number one/two") gives an implied progress bar.
- An open loop: promise the explanation early, pay it off late.
- The turn line at about 22 s, with a red background and shaking `BUT...`.
- A visual change every 2–4 s, anchored to keywords.
- One surprising number (a study result) in the fix scene.
- A CTA question that asks for a personal story. These get the most comments.

## Accuracy rules
- Only use explanations with real research behind them. Prefer textbook-level findings and well-known studies.
- Round honestly ("about a quarter"), and hedge where the science is debated.
- No medical advice. For mental-health-adjacent topics, keep it about normal everyday experience.
- Record 2–4 sources for the upload kit.
